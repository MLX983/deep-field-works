# Issue #31 long-form POC v1

- **Issue:** #31 — “Market expects infra results”
- **Preserved:** 2026-08-08
- **Status:** development evidence; nonpublic and noncanonical

Issue #31 was the first Deep Field Works attempt to combine three things in one bounded experiment:

- synthesis of multiple signals into one field report;
- a first DFW long-form editorial pattern;
- a complete rendered long-form page.

The experiment was intentionally reviewed as a proof of concept. It was not accepted as the future article template. The article and rendering are preserved because they reveal useful strengths, failure modes, and possible component needs before later compression or redesign.

## What worked

- The core thesis became stronger through research.
- The real site typography and layout remained coherent.
- Responsive rendering worked.
- The evidence posture was rigorous.
- The timing model made an abstract timing issue concrete.
- The counterargument was treated seriously.
- The related-topic concept proved useful.
- The private Tailscale review workflow worked.

## What overreached

- The article became far too long and research-report-like.
- It accumulated too many major sections.
- It carried too many numerical details.
- Inline source links overwhelmed the prose.
- Caveat density made the writing defensive.
- The research apparatus leaked into reader-facing content.
- The **Key distinction** and **Bounded conclusion** treatments felt invented and too report-like.
- The full eight-stage timing sequence consumed too much space.
- The reader-facing Issue #25 label exposed internal issue language.

## Presentation judgment

### Clear overreach

- heavy **Key distinction** box
- **Bounded conclusion** label and treatment
- exhaustive inline source-link treatment
- research-table/report density

### Promising but needs redesign

- timing/progression treatment
- counterargument/alternative-reading treatment
- related-topics footer

### New capability need

- restrained emphasis treatment
- public references/citation system
- related-topic system
- compact progression/sequence component

Do not treat any experimental component in this POC as canonical merely because it was implemented.

## Component-design boundary

Issue #31 demonstrated possible needs for a restrained key-point treatment, a counterargument or alternative-reading treatment, a compact progression or sequence treatment, a related-topics footer, and a citation/reference treatment.

No new component is approved by this record. The current POC implementations are not canonical. Figma exploration and human review must occur before Codex implements replacement components. Existing canonical Figma components and approved article examples remain authoritative until then.
