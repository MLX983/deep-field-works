# Issue #31 long-form POC v1 — presentation notes

- **Preserved:** 2026-08-08
- **Status:** development evidence; nonpublic and noncanonical
- **Scope:** human judgment on the rendered POC, not a specification for replacements

## Title and subtitle

The title and descriptive subtitle worked within the real Deep Field Works typography and page frame. They established the thesis and article posture without requiring a specialized treatment.

## Section-heading density

Nine major sections made the argument legible but turned the page into a report. The headings carried meaningful distinctions, yet their number made the reader traverse the research structure rather than a compressed public argument.

## Key distinction treatment

The opening proposition was worth emphasizing. The heavy labeled box was not. **Key distinction** felt like an invented report device and competed with the prose instead of quietly helping it.

**Human judgment:** clear overreach in its current form; the underlying need for restrained emphasis remains plausible.

## Numerical evidence density

The figures demonstrated a rigorous evidence base, but the accumulation of company-by-company spending, growth, cash-flow, backlog, and timing details made the article feel like an analyst report. Research-table density escaped into the public reading layer.

**Human judgment:** clear overreach.

## Inline citations

Twenty-three linked sources made provenance visible, including a useful distinction between primary or institutional evidence and market-reaction reporting. Repeated long source-title links interrupted sentence rhythm and overwhelmed the prose.

**Human judgment:** the evidence practice was strong; the exhaustive inline treatment was clear overreach. A public citation/reference capability is needed, but its visual behavior remains open for Figma exploration.

## Efficiency callout and Bounded conclusion

The extracted sentence usefully bounded the efficiency claim. The **Bounded conclusion** label exposed internal editorial reasoning and made the page feel like a research report.

**Human judgment:** clear overreach in its current form. No replacement treatment is specified here.

## Eight-stage timing sequence

The sequence made the lag between commitment and accumulated cash return concrete. It proved that progression can clarify an otherwise abstract temporal argument. At full eight-stage length, it occupied too much horizontal and vertical space, especially relative to the article’s central point.

**Human judgment:** promising but needs redesign as a compact progression or sequence treatment.

## Counterargument treatment

The article took the alternative interpretation seriously, which improved the argument. The boxed section made that pressure visible but added another strong treatment to an already dense page.

**Human judgment:** promising but needs redesign. The need is an alternative-reading or counterargument treatment, not approval of the current box.

## Related-topic footer

The footer demonstrated that conceptual adjacency can be useful without making another artifact part of the evidence chain. The reader-facing label `Issue #25` exposed internal workflow language.

**Human judgment:** promising but needs redesign as a public related-topic system.

## Mobile density

Responsive behavior worked: the timing sequence reflowed and the page remained coherent. Responsiveness did not solve the underlying editorial density. Long sections, repeated numerical evidence, citations, callouts, and the full sequence still created a heavy mobile reading experience.

**Human judgment:** technically successful, editorially too dense.

## Boundary

This record does not define replacement components or visual specifications. No new component is approved. Figma exploration and human review must precede any implementation, and the existing canonical presentation system remains authoritative.
