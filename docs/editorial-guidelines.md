# Editorial Guidelines

## Purpose

Deep Field Works is a field journal, laboratory notebook, and publishing platform for exploring how humans and machines work together.

The goal is not to build an audience, optimize for SEO, chase trends, or establish authority.

The goal is to document observations, develop ideas, test hypotheses, and create a public record of evolving thought.

## Writing Style

* Use short paragraphs.
* Prefer clarity over sophistication.
* Avoid corporate language.
* Avoid AI hype language.
* Avoid marketing language.
* Avoid unnecessary jargon.
* Do not write as an expert delivering conclusions.
* Write as an observer reporting findings.

## Tone

The tone should be:

* Curious
* Analytical
* Grounded
* Reflective
* Direct

The tone should not be:

* Evangelical
* Alarmist
* Promotional
* Cynical
* Overly academic

## Evidence

Clearly distinguish between:

* Observation
* Interpretation
* Speculation

Do not present speculation as fact.

## Preferred Structure

Most articles should contain:

1. Observation
2. Why it matters
3. Open questions

The objective is often to frame interesting questions rather than provide definitive answers.

## Chronology

Publication dates matter.

The site is intended to preserve:

* Early ideas
* Primitive models
* Failed assumptions
* Dead ends
* Evolution of thinking

Do not rewrite history to make earlier work appear more correct than it was.

## Visual Formatting

* Use meaningful headings.
* Use bullets when they improve scanning.
* Use diagrams when they clarify relationships.
* Avoid walls of text.

## Audience

Assume the reader is intelligent but busy.

Every article should be understandable without specialized domain knowledge.
