# Content Types

These content types are intentionally distinct.

The purpose is not taxonomy for its own sake. The purpose is to help authors and agents choose the correct publishing format.

When deciding between content types:

* Field Notes prioritize speed and observation.
* Articles prioritize synthesis and explanation.
* Checkpoints prioritize consolidation and orientation.
* Research Notes prioritize exploration.
* Experiments prioritize demonstration and learning.
* Project Journals prioritize development history.

Choose the smallest content type that adequately communicates the idea.

Do not automatically promote Field Notes into Articles.

Do not force unfinished ideas into polished formats.


## Field Notes

Short observations.

Characteristics:

* Fast to publish
* Focused on a single observation
* Often triggered by current events
* May contain tentative interpretations

Typical length:

200–600 words

## Articles

More developed explorations.

Characteristics:

* Multiple observations
* Supporting examples
* Structured argument
* Open questions

Typical length:

800–2,000 words

## Checkpoints

Periodic summaries of an ongoing topic.

Purpose:

* Help readers catch up
* Consolidate previous work
* Document changes in thinking

Examples:

* AI Adoption Checkpoint
* Cognitive Institutions Checkpoint
* Agentic Systems Checkpoint

## Research Notes

Working material.

Characteristics:

* Incomplete
* Exploratory
* May contain fragments
* May later become articles

## Experiments

Descriptions of prototypes, workflows, and tests.

Characteristics:

* Explain the goal
* Explain the process
* Document results
* Document failures

## Project Journals

Progress logs.

Purpose:

* Capture development history
* Preserve decisions
* Record lessons learned

These are closer to laboratory notes than finished essays.
