```md
# Title

Opening observation.

State the observation clearly and quickly.

## Why this matters

Explain why the observation is interesting.

Prefer concrete examples over abstract claims.

## Supporting observations

Use short sections.

Use bullets where appropriate.

Avoid unnecessary exposition.

## Tension or implication

What changes if the observation is correct?

What becomes possible?

What becomes harder?

## Open question

End with a meaningful question, tension, or unresolved observation.

The objective is not always to reach a conclusion.

Sometimes the objective is to frame the problem more clearly.
```

## Field Notes

Field Notes should generally contain:

* Observation
* Context
* Implication

Target length:

200–600 words

## Articles

Articles should generally contain:

* Observation
* Why it matters
* Supporting examples
* Open question

Target length:

800–2,000 words

## Checkpoints

Checkpoints should generally contain:

* What has changed
* What has been learned
* What remains uncertain
* Links to related pieces

Target length:

500–1,500 words

## Editorial Reminder

Distinguish:

* Observation
* Interpretation
* Speculation

Do not present speculation as fact.

Chronology is a feature.

Preserve the evolution of thought.
