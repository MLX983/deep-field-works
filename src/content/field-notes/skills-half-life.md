# Skills half-life

AI adoption is often framed as a training problem. Employees need to learn AI skills so organizations can benefit from the tools. But AI changes so quickly that many tool-specific skills begin decaying before they become workplace habits.

## Why it may matter

AI skills have different half-lives. Tool tricks decay quickly. Workflow patterns last longer. Judgment, delegation, evaluation, and governance last longer still. The value does not simply disappear; it often migrates from the user’s explicit skill into the platform, interface, or organizational process.

A designer may spend years becoming fast in a particular prototyping tool, only to see that advantage shrink when the team changes tools or AI makes basic construction nearly automatic. The durable skill is recognizing which interaction needs to be prototyped, what level of fidelity is sufficient, what failure modes to test, and how to interpret the result.

## Current interpretation

**Speculation (not verified):**

The goal of AI training should not be to teach people the current tool. It should teach people how to notice when the boundary between human work and machine work has moved.

## Open question

Can organizations train for adaptation faster than AI systems absorb the skills being taught?
