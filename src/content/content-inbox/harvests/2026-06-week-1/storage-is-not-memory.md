# Harvest Candidate

## Title

Storage Is Not Memory

## Suggested Artifact Type

Essay

## Primary Domain

Cognitive Infrastructure

## Related Concepts

* Externalized cognition
* Memory systems
* Retrieval
* Cognitive institutions

## Why It Matters

Many knowledge systems accumulate information indefinitely while becoming progressively less useful.

The distinction between storage and memory may explain why.

## Larger Pattern It May Represent

The next generation of knowledge systems may be judged by their ability to influence present reasoning rather than preserve historical information.

## Confidence

High

## Candidate Strength

Strong

Contains a clear distinction, obvious reader relevance, and multiple pathways for development.

## Potential Opening Questions

* Why do most knowledge bases become graveyards of information?
* What is the difference between storing information and remembering it?
* If an archive contains everything, why does it still forget?
* What would it mean for a knowledge system to recognize what matters now?
* Why do people spend years collecting information they rarely use?

### Interpretations

* Memory is functional, not archival.
* A useful archive affects current reasoning.

### Speculations

* Future cognitive systems may prioritize relevance, synthesis, and challenge rather than storage volume.
* Memory may become a design discipline rather than a storage discipline.
