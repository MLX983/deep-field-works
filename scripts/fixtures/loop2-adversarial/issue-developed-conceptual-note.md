### Body

Working model: Broad access to the same assistant may raise a shared floor while context quality and articulated judgment still produce different ceilings. The distinction is not simply who has the tool, but who can provide the conditions that let it do useful work.

Mechanism: A person who can name the decision, expose relevant constraints, and judge a plausible output gives the system a better working context. The assistant may then extend that judgment, while a thin request leaves it to guess which tradeoffs matter.

For example, two managers can use the same planning assistant. One supplies the decision history, constraints, and known disagreement; the other asks for a plan without that context. The interface is equal, but the resulting leverage may not be.

For example, two writers can begin with the same drafting tool. One can identify the audience, unresolved tension, and evidence boundary; the other supplies only a topic. The difference appears in the quality of the framing before any prose is generated.

Possible claim: Unequal leverage may persist even when access becomes widespread, but social strata should remain a working lens rather than a demonstrated hierarchy.

Open question: Which forms of context and articulated judgment remain durable when the underlying model changes?
